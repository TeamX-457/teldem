The problem

You only find out something went wrong after it's already a loss.

Across Nigerian homes and businesses, the things that matter most — food, fuel, vehicles, and physical spaces — are monitored reactively, if they're monitored at all. By the time you notice, the damage is done.

{% include "partials/icons/fridge.html" with cls="h-6 w-6 text-teldem-blue" %}

Food spoilage nobody caught in time

A fridge running warm overnight, a freezer door left ajar — small failures that quietly destroy stock, whether it's a household's weekly shop or a restaurant's entire cold chain.

{% include "partials/icons/gas.html" with cls="h-6 w-6 text-teldem-gold-600" %}

Gas that runs out at the worst moment

Without a way to see remaining levels, cooking gas runs dry mid-meal at home, or mid-service in a commercial kitchen — always at the least convenient time.

{% include "partials/icons/vehicle.html" with cls="h-6 w-6 text-teldem-blue" %}

Vehicles left vulnerable while unattended

Theft and damage often happen in the gaps — a delivery van overnight, a car in a compound — with no record of what happened or a way to be alerted in the moment.

{% include "partials/icons/home.html" with cls="h-6 w-6 text-teldem-green" %}

Homes and offices with no situational awareness

Motion, smoke, and access events go unnoticed without a system watching continuously — leaving people to rely on luck rather than information.
